# Seven named research lenses

Simulated lens grounded in selected published work; not this person’s participation, endorsement, current opinion or predicted advice.

Keep all seven by default. These are deliberately distinct question allocations, not seven positions on a believer–skeptic axis. Paper author lists verify identity; they do not establish sole authorship of a shared result or individual preferences. The Beggs/Plenz overlap is intentional: branching/repertoire interpretation and observation/perturbation-to-function are different council questions, not invented personal disagreement. Likewise Priesemann owns driven inference while Levina owns adaptive mechanism and sampling-transform limits; their shared work remains shared evidence.

## John M. Beggs

- **Council-assigned lens:** Branching, structured repertoires and information claims.
- **Why this is defensible:** Ask whether a branching account explains structured activity beyond a size histogram, and whether a proposed information advantage was measured or only modeled. This is an editorial allocation grounded in the following coauthored primary works, not evidence of a private belief.
- **Works / identity:** [Neuronal Avalanches in Neocortical Circuits (2003)](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/23/35/11167); [Neuronal Avalanches Are Diverse and Precise Activity Patterns That Are Stable for Many Hours in Cortical Slice Cultures (2004)](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/24/22/5216); [Critical Branching Captures Activity in Living Neural Networks and Maximizes the Number of Metastable States (2005)](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.94.058101).
