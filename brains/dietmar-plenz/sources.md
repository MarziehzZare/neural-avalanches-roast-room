## BP03 — Neuronal Avalanches in Neocortical Circuits (2003)

- **Authors / identity evidence:** John M. Beggs and Dietmar Plenz. [Primary author list](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/23/35/11167)
- **Version:** Published journal article, Journal of Neuroscience 23:11167–11177 (2003). [Record](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/23/35/11167)
- **Access / reading level:** Read the publisher abstract, introduction and Materials and Methods excerpt; extraction capped at 16,000 characters. Not a complete full-text audit.
- **Finding read:** The rat organotypic-culture and acute-slice LFP study reports avalanche-size scaling near a −3/2 exponent and a branching estimate near one; the information-transmission optimum comes from a feedforward simulation. [Exact source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/23/35/11167)
- **Method or abstract detail read:** Spontaneous negative LFP events were extracted from 60-channel arrays; thresholds, temporal bins and spatial rescaling are explicit parts of the measurement procedure. [Exact source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/23/35/11167)
- **Inference boundary (editorial assessment of this source):** Preparation-specific LFP results and a simulation do not by themselves establish human SOC, single-neuron causal ancestry, or behavioral benefit. [Evidence being bounded](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/23/35/11167)

## BP04 — Neuronal Avalanches Are Diverse and Precise Activity Patterns That Are Stable for Many Hours in Cortical Slice Cultures (2004)

- **Authors / identity evidence:** John M. Beggs and Dietmar Plenz. [Primary author list](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/24/22/5216)
- **Version:** Published journal article, Journal of Neuroscience 24:5216–5229 (2004). [Record](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/24/22/5216)
- **Access / reading level:** Read publisher abstract, introduction and acquisition/filtering/event-extraction methods excerpt; extraction capped at 13,000 characters.
- **Finding read:** Repeated spatiotemporal LFP avalanche families in rat slice cultures remained stable across hours and exhibited millisecond temporal precision. [Exact source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/24/22/5216)
- **Method or abstract detail read:** The study used correlation-based clustering and jitter analysis; the abstract describes continuous 10-hour recordings, while the methods explain the choice of 4-ms bins and −3-SD event thresholds. [Exact source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/24/22/5216)
- **Inference boundary (editorial assessment of this source):** The proposed memory-substrate interpretation is not a demonstration of learned memory content, behavioral memory, or a selective causal memory benefit. [Evidence being bounded](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/24/22/5216)

## SP09 — Neuronal Avalanches Imply Maximum Dynamic Range in Cortical Networks at Criticality (2009)

- **Authors / identity evidence:** Woodrow L. Shew, Hongdian Yang, Thomas Petermann, Rajarshi Roy and Dietmar Plenz. [Primary author list](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/49/15595)
- **Version:** Published journal article, Journal of Neuroscience 29:15595–15600 (2009). [Record](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/49/15595)
- **Access / reading level:** Read publisher abstract, introduction, methods and beginning of results; extraction capped at 16,000 characters. Supplements not audited.
- **Finding read:** Rat cortex slice-culture networks showed maximal stimulus-response dynamic range under conditions generating neuronal avalanches; pharmacological excitation/inhibition changes generated a tuning curve. [Exact source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/49/15595)
- **Method or abstract detail read:** Spontaneous LFP cluster statistics were compared with evoked input-output curves; dynamic range used stimulus intensities at 10% and 90% of the response range. The κ statistic compares an empirical size CDF with a reference avalanche CDF. [Exact source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/49/15595)
- **Inference boundary (editorial assessment of this source):** Manipulating excitation/inhibition affects multiple processes; this is stronger than merely observing spontaneous scaling, but does not isolate avalanches as the sole causal mediator or prove general cognitive optimization. [Evidence being bounded](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/49/15595)
