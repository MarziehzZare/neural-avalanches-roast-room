## P14 — Spike avalanches in vivo suggest a driven, slightly subcritical brain state (2014)

- **Authors / identity evidence:** Viola Priesemann, Michael Wibral, Mario Valderrama, Robert Pröpper, Michel Le Van Quyen, Theo Geisel, Jochen Triesch, Danko Nikolić and Matthias H. J. Munk. [Primary author list](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2014.00108/full)
- **Version:** Published journal article, Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience 8:108 (2014). [Record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2014.00108/full)
- **Access / reading level:** Read abstract, introduction, binning/subsampling figure explanation and early results/model description; extraction capped at 16,000 characters.
- **Finding read:** Recordings from rats, monkeys and cats, plus human LFPs, were compared with models; combined subsampling, drive and slightly subcritical propagation better matched the analyzed observables than separated critical avalanches. [Exact source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2014.00108/full)
- **Method or abstract detail read:** Avalanche size distributions, branching estimates, mean avalanche size and single-spike frequency were compared across bin sizes; the article explains merging and splitting under drive and subsampling. [Exact source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2014.00108/full)
- **Inference boundary (editorial assessment of this source):** The result is a preparation/model comparison, not a universal claim that no brain can be critical. Potential functional advantages in the abstract are interpretations, not an experimentally established cognitive or clinical benefit. [Evidence being bounded](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2014.00108/full)

## WP18 — Inferring collective dynamical states from widely unobserved systems (2018)

- **Authors / identity evidence:** Jens Wilting and Viola Priesemann. [Primary author list](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04725-4)
- **Version:** Published journal article, Nature Communications 9:2325 (2018). [Record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04725-4)
- **Access / reading level:** Read abstract, introduction and multistep-regression derivation/results excerpt including equations (1) and (2); extraction capped at 16,000 characters. Supplements not audited.
- **Finding read:** Multistep regression estimates propagation dynamics under spatial subsampling; application to rat, cat and monkey recordings identifies a reverberating regime between asynchronous-irregular and critical limits. [Exact source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04725-4)
- **Method or abstract detail read:** Under a first-order autoregressive conditional mean and suitable sampling, lagged regression slopes have form r_k=b m^k, separating sampling amplitude from the dynamical decay. The paper includes checks for nonstationarity and approximation validity. [Exact source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04725-4)
- **Inference boundary (editorial assessment of this source):** Subsampling invariance is conditional on the process and observation assumptions; m is an effective dynamical parameter, not proof of individual causal synaptic descendants or a universal SOC detector. [Evidence being bounded](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04725-4)

## LP17 — Subsampling scaling (2017)

- **Authors / identity evidence:** Anna Levina and Viola Priesemann. [Primary author list](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15140)
- **Version:** Published journal article, Nature Communications 8:15140 (2017). [Record](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15140)
- **Access / reading level:** Read abstract, introduction, mathematical-subsampling section and scaling derivation/model excerpt; extraction capped at 16,000 characters. Supplements not audited.
- **Finding read:** A subsampling-scaling framework distinguishes observation-fraction effects from finite-size effects and is applied to avalanche models and developing neural networks. [Exact source](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15140)
- **Method or abstract detail read:** Mathematical subsampling independently retains cluster events with probability p; one full cluster maps to one observed cluster, possibly empty. Approximate p-scaling is analyzed for power-law tails and cutoffs; small-size deviations and conditions for exponential distributions are explicit. [Exact source](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15140)
- **Inference boundary (editorial assessment of this source):** The mathematical observation map need not match real binning: missing events can split cascades. Longer recording does not recover unobserved units, and apparent collapse alone is not universal proof of criticality. [Evidence being bounded](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15140)
